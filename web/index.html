What am I looking at?

Underground, water or oil seeps through rock. How easily it moves depends on the rock's permeability: sand lets fluid through, clay barely does. Here fluid is injected evenly everywhere in a square block of rock and drains away at its edges. The question is what pressure builds up inside.

A neural network (a Fourier neural operator) answers in a few milliseconds. A conventional solver computes the same answer the slow, reliable way, so you can see how far off the network is. The network was trained on one kind of rock. Try others below and watch where it stops being trustworthy, and why.

Maps: light rock is permeable, dark is tight. Pressure goes from dark (low) to yellow (high). The error map shows where the network is too high (red) or too low (blue).

Screen many rocks at once

When you need answers for many fields, solving each one conventionally is the expensive part. Here the network predicts every field, and a cheap check ranks them by how wrong the network probably is. The solver then checks only the riskiest few, plus a random sample that tells you how good the unchecked answers are.
